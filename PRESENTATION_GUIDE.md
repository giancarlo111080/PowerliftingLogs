# Iron Forge Presentation Guide

## Audience and Goal

This walkthrough is designed for a joint discussion with a coach and a lifter. The goal is to show how one training plan moves through the full coaching cycle:

1. The coach builds and assigns the plan.
2. The lifter accepts it and records actual training.
3. Both review progress and communicate in context.
4. The system highlights signals, while the coach keeps final decision authority.

Allow about 25-35 minutes for the full walkthrough. Use one athlete with an active program, several completed and skipped sets, a recovery check-in, and one linked video before presenting.

## Opening Script

> Iron Forge is a shared powerlifting workspace for programming, training logs, readiness, and review. The coach and lifter see different tools, but they work from the same plan and the same recorded training. I will first show the lifter's daily workflow, then switch to the coach's programming and review workflow.

## Navigation and Roles

**Show:** The sidebar, role label, athlete selector, notification bell, and theme control.

**Say:**

> Access is role-based. A lifter sees personal training and performance tools. A coach sees roster, programming, intelligence, and review tools. When a coach selects an athlete here, the shared screens update to that athlete's context. An account with both permissions can switch between My training and Coaching.

**Discuss:** Should assistant coaches or other staff have a third permission level, or are coach and lifter roles enough?

# Shared Tabs

## 1. Dashboard

### Lifter View

**Purpose:** Give the lifter an immediate answer to three questions: What am I doing next? How is the block going? Is there anything from my coach that needs action?

**Show:**

- A pending program offer, if available, and the Accept/Decline actions.
- Readiness, adherence, and completed volume.
- The next workout and **Open training log** button.
- Current squat, bench, and deadlift baselines.
- Recent achievements and top-set footage.
- The offline/local save status.

**Say:**

> The lifter dashboard is action-first. It puts the next workout beside the current readiness, adherence, and completed volume. A coach's new program does not silently replace the active plan: the lifter accepts or declines it, and previous training history remains preserved.

**Ask the lifter:** Is this enough information before training, or would you want warm-ups, session duration, or coach notes visible here?

### Coach View

**Purpose:** Triage the roster and identify who needs attention before opening individual records.

**Show:**

- Assigned athletes, athletes active this week, attention queue, and completed reviews.
- Athlete invitation by email or registration link.
- Roster status with readiness, adherence, last session, and attention reason.
- Shortcuts to Programs and Program Review.

**Say:**

> The coach dashboard is a triage desk. Instead of opening every athlete, the coach can see who has low readiness, new footage, pending work, or no active program. Selecting a lifter changes the active review context throughout the coach workspace.

**Ask the coach:** Which status should be most prominent when you begin your day: readiness, missed work, new video, or proximity to competition?

## 2. Training Log

**Audience:** Coach and lifter.

**Purpose:** Serve as the shared source of truth for prescribed work and actual work.

**Show:**

- Program selection and scheduled-day navigation.
- Exercise prescriptions using RPE, RIR, percentage, or exact load.
- Recording actual weight, repetitions, RPE, rest, and set completion.
- Classifying missed work as failed, interrupted, rescheduled, pain-limited, equipment-related, or other.
- Session effort rating from 1-10.
- Instagram footage or lift-video analysis attached to a set.
- Day-level comments between coach and lifter.
- Schedule controls for moving one workout or a full week.
- Coach controls for editing the live plan, adding work, or duplicating weeks and days.

**Say:**

> This is the center of the product. The prescription and the result stay together. A completed set records what actually happened, while a skipped set records why it did not happen. That distinction gives the coach better information than a simple completion percentage. Comments and footage stay attached to the relevant training day or set.

> The coach can adjust the live plan without changing the reusable master template. The lifter can move training when life disrupts the schedule, and the system records who changed the date.

**Ask both:** What is the minimum information a lifter will reliably enter after every working set? Which fields should remain optional?

**Demo caution:** Avoid deleting a program, week, day, or exercise during the live presentation unless the demo data is disposable.

## 3. Analytics

**Audience:** Coach and lifter.

**Purpose:** Turn completed training records into a shared weekly review.

**Show:**

- Program selector.
- Completed tonnage, adherence, and readiness estimate.
- Weekly volume.
- Done, skipped, and remaining sets.
- Heaviest completed squat, bench, and deadlift sets.
- Session effort trend.
- Fatigue and readiness trend.

**Say:**

> Analytics uses logged work, not planned estimates. Volume comes from completed sets with actual loads. Adherence separates completed, skipped, and remaining work. The readiness number combines training completion, load, and session ratings as a planning aid.

> Readiness is not a diagnosis and should not overrule pain, technique, or the coach's judgment. Its value is consistency: it gives both people the same signal to discuss.

**Ask the coach:** Which trend would actually change next week's prescription? Ask the lifter: Which chart helps you understand your own progress without creating unnecessary pressure?

## 4. Profile

**Audience:** Coach and lifter, with richer competition and career fields for the lifter.

**Purpose:** Maintain the baseline information that drives classification, programming context, and communication preferences.

**Show:**

- Name and personal details.
- Country, date of birth, sex, age division, experience, and equipment.
- Body weight, weight class, and squat/bench/deadlift 1RM baselines.
- Upcoming meet and federation information.
- Athlete program history and active-block selection, when available.
- Federation membership and authorized OpenPowerlifting identity linking.
- Notification preferences and coaching relationship controls.

**Say:**

> The profile is more than an account page. These baselines give context to prescriptions, weight-class decisions, and competition planning. The athlete controls personal identity links, notification settings, and the coaching relationship.

**Ask both:** Who should be allowed to edit 1RM baselines and competition details: the lifter, the coach, or both with an audit history?

# Lifter-Only Tab

## 5. Performance Hub

**Purpose:** Give the lifter a private space for recovery, strength trends, technique history, meet-day preparation, and data control.

**Show:**

- Daily check-in for sleep, soreness, stress, pain, motivation, body weight, and optional notes.
- Current readiness and check-in history count.
- Projected squat, bench, and deadlift ranges with confidence and sample size.
- Technique timeline from compatible video analyses.
- Meet Day Command Center and attempt planning.
- Sync status and performance-data export.
- Separate consent controls for operations, model training, and video-model training.

**Say:**

> The lifter records subjective recovery signals before training. The app turns those inputs into a consistent readiness signal for discussion, not medical advice. Pain at four or above is explicitly flagged for human review.

> Strength projections are ranges, not promises. Confidence depends on recent valid top sets, and low-confidence video measurements are withheld rather than presented as precise data.

> The athlete can export performance data, revoke optional model-training consent, and delete performance records. Video-model consent is separate and off by default.

**Ask the lifter:** Would you complete this check-in daily? Which question feels useful, and which feels too intrusive? Ask the coach: At what threshold would you contact the athlete before training?

# Coach-Only Tabs

## 6. Athletes

**Purpose:** Provide a roster-level comparison and set the athlete being reviewed.

**Show:**

- Each lifter's weight class and active program.
- Readiness, adherence, and training status.
- Selecting a lifter and confirming that the sidebar review context changes.

**Say:**

> This view helps the coach scan the roster without mixing athlete records. Selecting a row sets that lifter as the active context for Analytics, Training Log, Intelligence Desk, and Program Review.

**Ask the coach:** What roster filters would matter most at scale: competition date, training phase, missed sessions, pain flags, or unread feedback?

## 7. Programs

**Purpose:** Build reusable master templates and assign a dated copy to an athlete.

**Show:**

- Creating a template with phase, goal, number of weeks, and training days per week.
- Expanding weeks and training days.
- Adding squat, bench, deadlift, and accessory exercises.
- Setting sets, repetitions, and RPE/RIR/percentage/exact-load prescriptions.
- Duplicating weeks or days.
- Sharing a template with another coach.
- Assigning the template to an athlete with a start date.

**Say:**

> Programs separates reusable coaching IP from an athlete's live plan. The coach builds a master template, then assigns a dated copy. Later changes to the master do not rewrite an athlete's training history. This allows reuse without losing individualization.

**Ask the coach:** Which part of program creation takes the most time today? Would week duplication, exercise libraries, or bulk athlete assignment save the most effort?

**Demo caution:** Prepare a small two-week template. Building a complete block live will consume the meeting and hide the workflow.

## 8. Intelligence Desk

**Purpose:** Prioritize exceptions and support accountable coaching decisions without automating away the coach.

**Show:**

- Exceptions such as pain, low readiness, missed work, RPE overshoots, workload spikes, comments, and new video.
- Evidence-based adaptive recommendations with confidence and uncertainty.
- Approve or reject controls and the required coaching rationale.
- Decision journal, review date, expected outcome, and later outcome review.
- Program versions, semantic differences, and rollback as a new version.
- Timestamped video notes.
- Athlete groups, bulk template assignment, and exercise library.

**Say:**

> The Intelligence Desk does not make silent changes. It gathers exceptions and produces deterministic recommendations with evidence, confidence, uncertainty, and contraindications. The coach approves or rejects the recommendation and records why.

> Every intervention can have an expected outcome and review date. Later, the coach records whether it improved, worsened, or did not change the result. Program rollback creates a new version instead of erasing history.

**Ask the coach:** What evidence would you require before trusting a recommendation? Which actions should always require explicit approval?

**Safety statement:**

> Pain and readiness signals support human review. They do not diagnose injury, prescribe medical treatment, or replace qualified clinical care.

## 9. Program Review

**Purpose:** Conduct a focused review of one lifter's current block.

**Show:**

- Active athlete, active program, and upcoming meet.
- Readiness decision guidance.
- Set completion and squat/bench/deadlift baselines.
- Program progress.
- Form flags, linked footage, and athlete comments.
- Completing items in the review queue.

**Say:**

> Program Review is the coach's focused check-in for one lifter. It brings the plan, adherence, readiness, baselines, footage, and comments into one decision screen. Completing an item clears the current review queue so the coach can separate reviewed work from new work.

**Ask both:** Would this screen work for a weekly coach-lifter call? What decision or note should be captured before the review is considered complete?

# Notification Center

**Access:** Open from the bell in the header for either role.

**Purpose:** Convert important events into a prioritized action list.

**Show:**

- All, unread, training, coaching, meet, and sync filters.
- Mark all as read and dismiss actions.
- Opening an alert to navigate to the relevant screen.
- Retry synchronization when a sync alert exists.

**Say:**

> Notifications are generated from the current workspace: upcoming or incomplete workouts, recovery check-ins, program offers, schedule changes, coaching items, meet milestones, and sync failures. Each alert links to the place where the user can act.

**Ask both:** Which alerts deserve immediate attention, and which should be grouped into a daily summary?

# Recommended Live Demo Order

1. Sign in as the coach and explain role-based navigation.
2. **Dashboard:** select the demo athlete and review the attention queue.
3. **Programs:** show a prepared template and assign it.
4. Switch to the lifter workspace.
5. **Dashboard:** accept the offered program.
6. **Training Log:** complete one set, skip one with a reason, rate the session, and leave a comment.
7. **Performance Hub:** save a recovery check-in.
8. **Analytics:** show how the new records affect adherence, volume, and readiness.
9. Switch back to the coach workspace.
10. **Program Review:** show the lifter's progress and new review item.
11. **Intelligence Desk:** discuss an exception or recommendation and its approval trail.
12. Open **Notifications**, then finish at **Profile** with consent and data ownership.

## Closing Script

> The main idea is a closed feedback loop. The coach creates the plan, the lifter records reality, and both review the same evidence. The system organizes signals and suggests where to look, but the coach and lifter remain responsible for the decision.

> I would like your feedback on three things: what would save you time every week, what information you would genuinely keep up to date, and what would need to change before you trusted this in real coaching.

## Final Discussion Questions

1. Which existing tool or spreadsheet would this replace?
2. Where does your current coach-lifter communication break down?
3. Which data is essential, optional, or too burdensome to record?
4. What should happen when the lifter and coach disagree about a program change?
5. Which readiness or pain signals should trigger a conversation rather than an automatic action?
6. What would make the platform trustworthy enough for meet preparation?

## Presenter Notes

- Use the terms **readiness estimate**, **planning aid**, and **strength projection**. Avoid presenting them as diagnoses or guarantees.
- Explain that the web demo uses local-first storage and can queue synchronization. Confirm the target deployment configuration before promising cross-device real-time updates.
- Present video analysis as a review aid. Measurement availability depends on camera angle, sample compatibility, and confidence.
- Keep one clean backup athlete and program in case live edits make the primary demo state difficult to follow.
- Do not expose real email addresses, private health notes, federation IDs, or invitation links during a recorded presentation.
